## Scientific datasets

- [NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory](https://doi.org/10.15138/yaf1-bk21) provides the monthly Mauna Loa CO₂ observations under CC0-1.0.
- [Palmer Station LTER and Kristen Gorman](https://allisonhorst.github.io/palmerpenguins/) collected the penguin observations. The palmerpenguins data package by Horst, Hill, and Gorman provides the CC0-1.0 snapshot used here.
- [Steel Plates Faults](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5J88N) by M. Buscema, S. Terzi, and W. Tastle (2010), UCI Machine Learning Repository, is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The example adapts four feature columns across 1,941 records to compute a Pearson correlation matrix.
- [Glass Identification](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5WW2P) by B. German (1987), UCI Machine Learning Repository, is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The PCA example retains all 214 specimen IDs and nine measurements from `glass.data`, describes the six observed class codes with readable labels, and standardizes the measurements to compute a principal component biplot. The code example uses six source specimens and three measurements.
- The spatial defect map uses three measured wafer grids from [MIR-WM811K (2015), MIR Lab corpus](https://web.archive.org/web/20260609012331/http://mirlab.org/dataset/public/), copyright 2015 Jyh-Shing Roger Jang. [Wu, Jang, and Chen (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSM.2014.2364237) describes the dataset. The chart restores zero-based row and column indices from the [pinned JSON export](https://github.com/wafertools/wafermap/blob/28a2479e67ba601aff3a70a6809a8c7d46f5fe0c/docs/examples/wm811k-sample.json) and retains every pass/fail observation, source wafer index, lot, and pattern label in the three selected wafers. The [original dataset terms](licenses/WM-811K.txt) permit redistribution with its notice and citations.
- [Processing a maize germ side stream](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lwt.2025.118467) by K. Wetterauw, Y. Yi, E. van der Linden, R. M. Boom, P. Wilms, P. Venema, and M. A. I. Schutyser (2025) is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The Pareto example uses [Table 1](https://edepot.wur.nl/700871) for the three active conditions. It keeps the reported specific energy, inverts the reported specific pressing time to get feed rate, and takes the complement of the residual cake oil yield to describe the proportion of feed oil removed.
- [AISI 316L turning experiments](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7892469) by Hiovanis Castillo, Alexis Cordovés García, Roberto Pérez Rodríguez, José Antonio Yarza Acuña, and Ricardo Lorenzo Avila Rondon (2023) are available under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The Pareto example uses all 54 runs in `Data experimental.xls`. It retains measured surface roughness and derives nominal material removal rate and cutting-interface specific energy from the recorded settings and force using the [original turning study](https://doi.org/10.3390/met11111698).
- [NIST's Heat Flow Meter case study](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section4/eda4281.htm) provides Bob Zarr's calibration-factor measurements.
- [Adeel Zafar's diffraction data](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/ACPWBG) supplies the DyNiC₂ temperature series from Harvard Dataverse V1 under CC0-1.0. The peak tracker derives local pseudo-Voigt fits from three windows in the same measured scans.
- Björn Ehlig, Ole Lessmann, Kevin S. Jewell, and Arne Wick, caffeine tandem mass spectra at [40 V](https://github.com/MassBank/MassBank-data/blob/72980b3af504cfd1e89ac54d7698d3da45d85a58/BAFG/MSBNK-BAFG-CSL2311094663.txt) and [60 V](https://github.com/MassBank/MassBank-data/blob/72980b3af504cfd1e89ac54d7698d3da45d85a58/BAFG/MSBNK-BAFG-CSL2311094661.txt), MassBank 2026.11. The records are © 2026 Federal Institute of Hydrology (Bundesanstalt für Gewässerkunde), Koblenz, Germany, under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The mirrored spectrum stores the published m/z and intensity columns, scales each spectrum to its own base peak, and computes peak pairs within the selected m/z tolerance. The guide's code sample uses a subset of the same peaks.
- [CMS Run2010B dimuon events](https://doi.org/10.7483/OPENDATA.CMS.CB8H.MFFA) by Thomas McCauley (2014), CERN Open Data Portal, is released under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). The example counts the 100,000 published invariant mass values into a histogram.
- [Bearing Vibration Data under Time-varying Rotational Speed Conditions, version 2](https://doi.org/10.17632/v43hmbwxpm.2) by Huan Huang and Natalie Baddour (2019), University of Ottawa, is available under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The Campbell diagram converts the H-A-1 and I-A-1 accelerometer and encoder recordings into relative vibration spectra indexed by measured shaft speed. The wavelet scalogram transforms a filtered and normalized excerpt of the I-A-1 accelerometer record into relative time–frequency power. Its original waveform asset stores the corresponding 512,000 Channel_1 samples at the published 200 kHz in lossless form. The bearing envelope example derives broadband power and angularly resampled envelope spectra from H-A-1 and I-A-1.
- [Vibration signals of different fault conditions in a generic rotating machine](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14388093) by Amanda Lucatto Marra and Claudio Garcia (2024), Universidade de São Paulo, is available under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The coherence and phase comparison derives cross-spectral estimates from the paired horizontal accelerometer channels in two 30 Hz test-rig recordings.
- [Data for “Coherent Optical Clock Down-Conversion for Microwave Frequencies with 10⁻¹⁸ Instability”](https://doi.org/10.18434/M32206) by Takuma Nakamura et al. (2020), National Institute of Standards and Technology, is available under [NIST data terms](https://www.nist.gov/open/license). PITANT derived overlapping Allan deviations on November 23, 2026, by converting the measured phase to time error, differencing the synchronized records for the residual, and aggregating all three series over discrete averaging times. The original data are provided as is under NIST’s published terms.
- [Damped Pendulum Dataset](https://github.com/EnzeXu/Damped_Pendulum_Dataset/tree/cbf82673641ecd65e902ad5c38a048387649a2a0) by Enze Xu et al. is licensed under [Apache-2.0](licenses/Damped-Pendulum-Dataset-Apache-2.0.txt). The phase-plane example excerpts three 0.607 m trials from release, rebases their timestamps, and retains every fourth sensor sample.
- [Room temperature and elevated temperature tensile test and elastic properties data of Al-alloy EN AW-2618A after different aging times and temperatures](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10377164) by Philipp von Hartrott and Birgit Skrotzki (2023), Fraunhofer IWM and BAM, is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The stress–strain example retains measured points from three room-temperature tensile curves in their original order, subsets them by strain, and fits the initial slopes over a common strain interval. It reports elongation after fracture A from the corresponding specimen rows in the tensile test overview.
- [Dataset for Hyperelastic Material Parameter Identification of a Polymer Using the Virtual Fields Method and Uniaxial Tensile Tests](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18617429) by Marcin Nowak, Paweł Szeptyński, Sandra Musiał, and Michał Maj (2026) is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The full-field strain example derives axial strain from the measured displacement fields, selects a reduced reference grid and time sequence, and links the maps to the synchronized measured force.
- [The FABEST database: A benchmark fatigue dataset on 42CrMo4 +QT steel](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23603840) by Jan Papuga et al. (2026), 23 October snapshot, is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The fatigue S–N example extracts the measured cyclic tests from FAB060 and FAB070, retains their stress amplitudes and cycle counts, and maps documented complete breaks and interrupted tests to failure and runout markers.
- [Comparative Evaluation of Fatigue Crack Propagation in High-Strength Steels S460, S690, and S960 Using Optical and CMOD Methods at Stress Ratio R = 0.1](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21901500) by Stanislav Seitl, Mohammad Sami Al Khazali, Pavel Pokorný, Lucie Malíková, Grzegorz Stanislaw Lesiuk, and Szymon Dziuba (2026) is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The crack-growth example extracts the workbook's selected crack mouth opening displacement measurements, sorts them by stress intensity range, and fits a shared demonstration interval.
- [ORCHID-Diagrams v2026.10](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23735859) © 2026 Raymundo Arróyave, James E. Saal, and Dongwon Shin, under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), supplies the Ag–Cu equilibrium calculated with pycalphad 0.11.2. The calculation uses `AgCu-00Moo.tdb` from [Bengt Hallstedt's SGTE collection](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.calphad.2025.102833) and the [original assessment by K.-W. Moon et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11664-000-0003-x). The example selects six calculated branches and their invariant, converts temperature to °C and mole fraction to atomic percent, and clips the solvus curves at 300 °C by linear interpolation.
- [A Study on the Mechanical Properties of Engineered Geopolymer Composites Based on Red Mud, Fly Ash, and Slag](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym18182271) by Shuo Xu, Yu Ling, Xin Luo, Jingyuan Wang, Yicong Zhong, and Gai Chen (2026) is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The ternary example joins ten mixture proportions and measured mean compressive strengths from Tables 2 and 3, converts each precursor dosage to a fraction of the total dry binder mass, and fits a Scheffé quadratic to those observations.
- [High-Resolution XCT image data of additively manufactured cobalt-chromium samples](https://doi.org/10.18434/M32162) by Felix H. Kim, Edward J. Garboczi, Shawn P. Moylan, and John Slotwinski (2020), National Institute of Standards and Technology, is provided as is under [NIST data terms](https://www.nist.gov/open/license). PITANT adapted the data on November 23, 2026, by cropping registered reconstructed and segmented Sample 2 slices at native voxel spacing, quantizing reconstructed intensity for display, and numbering four enclosed pore components. The original segmentation method is documented in the authors’ [2017 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addma.2017.06.011).
- [Overhang Part X4, AMMT XCT data](https://doi.org/10.18434/mds2-2291) by Maxwell Praniewicz, Brandon Lane, Felix Kim, and Christopher Saldana (2020), National Institute of Standards and Technology, is provided as is under [NIST data terms](https://www.nist.gov/open/license). PITANT adapted the data on November 23, 2026, by refining Part 4’s datum alignment, spatially subsampling its XCT surface, computing signed distances to the original nominal triangles, and simplifying the nominal bore tessellation for display. The source acquisition and preprocessing are described in the authors’ [data paper](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.125.031).
- [CIE 1931 chromaticity coordinates of spectrum loci, 2 degree observer](https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.mifmy4x4), International Commission on Illumination (CIE), Vienna, 2019, is licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) per the [dataset metadata](https://files.cie.co.at/Publications-datasets/CIE_cc_1931_2deg.csv_metadata.json). The adapted data in `cie-1931-locus.json` retain that license and reproduce the wavelength, x and y columns at all 471 wavelengths. The gamut coordinates come from [W3C CSS Color 4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/#predefined) for sRGB and Display P3 and [ITU-R BT.2020-2](https://www.itu.int/rec/r-rec-bt.2020-2-201510-i/en) for Rec. 2020.
- [Spectral radiance factors of 99 test samples for the CIE colour fidelity index calculation, 1 nm table](https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.8svs5rqd), derived from the [original CIE 224:2017 sample table](https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.wi5idbqu), supplies spectra and material categories. PITANT multiplies the factors by [CIE standard illuminant D65](https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.hjfjmt59) and the [CIE 1931 2° colour-matching functions](https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.xvudnb9b), sums the products over 380–780 nm, and derives xy from the resulting XYZ coordinates. These source tables are published by CIE, Vienna, under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The adapted sample data in `cie-measured-samples.ts` retain that license.
- [1995 Cuprite radiative transfer ground-calibrated reflectance cube](https://popo.jpl.nasa.gov/1995_cuprite_RTGC_rfl_cube/), courtesy NASA/JPL-Caltech, with USGS surface reflectance calibration. [NASA Earthdata's data use guidance](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software-policies/data-use-guidance) provides unmarked NASA-led mission data under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). The hyperspectral example selects every third source row and column within a crop, sorts and selects 126 source wavelength channels, masks eight low-signal channels during display and analysis, and retains the original signed counts. See the [USGS calibration method](https://aviris.jpl.nasa.gov/proceedings/workshops/02_docs/2002_Clark_web.pdf) for the reflectance scale and atmospheric absorption limitations.
- [Effects of Thermal Treatment on the Physical Properties of Edible Calcium Alginate Gel Beads](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods8110578) by Seonghui Kim, Chungeun Jeong, Suengmok Cho, and Seon-Bong Kim (2019) is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The response surface example extracts the heating temperature, heating time, and measured mean sphericity for all eleven runs in Table 2 and fits an equal-weight quadratic in the published physical factors.
